The day runs out of options faster than it runs out of flights

Scheduled departure hour, 9am to 11pm. Disruption risk roughly triples across the day. The number of later same-route flights a stranded passenger could be moved onto collapses almost to zero well before that.

Same event, different hour, different outcome. A disruption at 9am still has 5.8 later flights it could move to. The same disruption at 8pm has 0.36. By 11pm, zero.

Delay cause and stranding risk don't move together evenly

Weather delays are rarer than carrier or aircraft-rotation delays, but they run longer and strand passengers more often than any other cause.

Frequency drives recovery more than reliability does. American disrupts more often than SkyWest, 16.8% vs. 15.1%, but strands passengers less, 4.1% vs. 6.1%, because it has more daily frequencies to rebook onto.

Lubbock isn't an outlier. It's a small station, and small stations strand worst

Worst-stranding airports with at least 5,000 flights in scope. Thin routes have no slack to absorb a failure, regardless of carrier.

Independent analysis of public Bureau of Transportation Statistics on-time performance data, June 2025 through May 2026, scoped to flights touching ORD, DEN, IAH, EWR, SFO, IAD, LAX, or Lubbock. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or using internal data from any airline.

"Recovery" here means a later same-day departure on the same origin-destination pair. It does not account for connecting itineraries, partner-airline rerouting, or ground transport, so it is a floor on real options, not the complete picture.
