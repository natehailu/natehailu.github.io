Airline Rebooking Flow Efficiency · Phase 2

Rebooking Flow Wireframes

Three defects observed during a five-hour irregular-operations event at a small-station gate, and the interface changes proposed to remove them. Screens are generic reconstructions drawn from firsthand use of the tooling, not any carrier's actual software.

The analysis behind this redesign found that recovery capacity collapses across the day. Among disrupted flights, 10.7% of 9am departures leave a passenger with no same-day option on their route. By 8pm that figure is 73.2%, and average remaining options fall from 5.8 to 0.36.

That is the context these defects sit inside. When the system also blocks a rebooking during the evening window, it is removing options from a passenger who has almost none left. The cost of a slow screen is not measured in minutes, it is measured in whether the passenger sleeps at home.

Target 1 · Critical

Deplane resets ticket status

A boarding scan marks a ticket used, by design and irreversibly. When passengers are involuntarily deplaned, no system state exists for "boarded, then removed." Every rebooking attempt is then blocked, and the only resolution observed was a phone call to employee support, up to 30 minutes per passenger.

Deplane event triggers the boarding-scan logic in reverse. Removes the blocked branch entirely, no phone call, no manual override.

Impact. Eliminates the largest time sink observed during the event. Every passenger needing a rebook was blocked by this, and the fix is a state transition the system already performs in one direction.

Target 2 · Secondary

Bag status at the point of action

Checked-bag status sits several clicks away from the rebook and cancel screen, and nothing prompts the agent to look. A passenger can walk away with a bag still on the aircraft and no one is aware. No ramp notification exists even when it is caught.

Bag state is surfaced inline at the moment of the action, with the ramp notification attached to the primary path. Proceeding without a pull stays possible but becomes an explicit choice.

Impact. Closes a gap that currently depends entirely on the agent remembering to ask, at the point in a disruption when they are least likely to.

Target 3 · Tertiary

Multi-leg rebook selection

The multi-leg rebook screen presents a checkbox for each flight segment. One missed or wrong checkbox silently redefines the itinerary, turning a connection city into a final destination. Each mistake cost roughly 15 to 20 minutes to unwind, and the errors compound under volume.

The defect is not the checkbox. It is that the screen never states the outcome. The agent selects segments and the system infers a trip, but the inferred trip is never shown back before it is committed.

The segment list still drives the change, but the screen now states the trip it produces, in plain language, before commit. A destination change raises its own notice rather than passing silently.

Impact. The error becomes visible at the moment it is made instead of 15 to 20 minutes later. The fix adds a confirmation state rather than replacing the selection mechanism, so it does not require retraining agents on a new interaction pattern during an irregular-operations event.

Defects documented from direct observation of a five-hour irregular-operations event: 38 passengers booked, 23 departed, one system defect, one interface defect, one process gap.
